Image 1 is the reference photo. Images 2-5 are the judge's four views of the current 3D model, on white, each
labelled: front-left 30°, front-right 30°, from the front 15° below, from the front 30° above.

The judge compares each view with the photo, side by side with a rival's model, on this scale: 3 = minor issues
(slight shape differences, missing small details), 5 = moderate issues (wrong style, significant details missing),
7 = major issues (wrong category but related). A model wins only when no view is clearly worse than the rival's:
one view with a moderate issue cancels wins in all the others.

For EACH view, list the problems that would cost a moderate penalty or worse, most harmful first, at most 4: what is
wrong, on which part, and the concrete fix (shape, proportion, count, position, colour or material, a missing part
or surface detail). Leave a view's list empty when it has no such problem; never invent problems. Ignore lighting,
background and render quality. The pipeline tips the object towards the camera and sets its size and centring, so
never ask for those; but do report a view where the object is turned differently from the photo (another side facing
the viewer, lying instead of standing, a side or front view where the photo shows three-quarters, mirrored left to
right), and say which way to turn it.
