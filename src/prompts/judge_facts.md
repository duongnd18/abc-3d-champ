HOW YOU ARE SCORED (measured facts — design for these):
- An automatic judge (a vision-language model) renders your module from 4 FIXED cameras and compares each
  render with the reference photo, side by side with a rival's render. Lower penalty wins.
- Cameras (object space, Y up, the object's FRONT faces +Z): front-left 30° and front-right 30° at object
  height; from the front 15° below; from the front 30° above. Neutral lighting, plain white background.
- What loses duels, most important first:
  1. Wrong SHAPE: silhouette, proportions, part sizes, missing or extra major parts.
  2. Bad FRAMING: object seen from an unhelpful side, a flat object seen edge-on, the photographed face hidden.
  3. "Simplified / stylized / cartoonish / low-poly / toy-like" — the single most common complaint in lost
     duels (43% of losses vs 23% of wins). The judge wants a REALISTIC PRODUCT.
  4. Wrong material/colour: wrong hue, too dark or washed out, matte where the photo is glossy.
  5. "Flat / 2D / no depth"; missing visible details the photo clearly shows (it names them).
- A duel is decided only when the four views agree; one view that prefers the rival (most often the one from 30°
  above, which shows tops, rims, openings and interiors) turns a win into a draw.
- Tie-break stages also look from the right, the back, the left and straight down, check a feature checklist
  (main object + 4-7 countable/shape/presence/colour features) and flag "paper-thin, empty, replaced by
  boxes/slabs" models. So the object must be complete and solid from EVERY side, back included.
- Hidden internals, animation and code style do not matter.
